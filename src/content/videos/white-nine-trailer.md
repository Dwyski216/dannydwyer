About fifty years ago, three Massachusetts teenagers, Joe, Christos, and Charlie, were paralyzed by spinal cord injuries, at a time when the world expected almost nothing from anyone in a wheelchair. They found each other, and together they refused to accept that.

White Nine follows their friendship across five decades and into the heart of Massachusetts’ independent living movement, part of the larger fight that led to the Americans with Disabilities Act.

It’s a history lesson that doesn’t stay in the past. The film makes the case that the ADA’s protections still need defending today, and that the work Joe, Christos, and Charlie started isn’t finished.

Pointing a camera at people who helped change what’s possible, and who are still fighting, is the kind of job you don’t forget.
